 2.1.  Overview  of  Shape  Memory  Alloys  and  Pseudoelasticity  Shape  Memory  Alloys  (SMAs)  are  a  unique  class  of  metallic  materials  that  exhibit  the  
ability
 
to

reversible,

solid-state

between
 
a
 
high-temperature
 
parent
 
phase,
 
known
 
as
 
austenite,
 
and
 
a
 
lower-temperature,
 
more
 
deformable
 
phase
 
called
 
martensite.
7
 Two  primary  functional  properties  arise  from  this  transformation:  the  
Shape
 
Memory
 
Effect
 
(SME)
 
and
 
Pseudoelasticity
 
(SE),
 
also
 
referred
 
to
 
as
 
superelasticity.
 
The  SME  involves  deforming  the  alloy  in  its  martensitic  state  and  then  recovering  the  
original
 
shape
 
upon
 
heating
 
above
 
a
 
critical
 
transformation
 
temperature
 
(austenite
 
finish
 
temperature,
 
Af ).
9
 Pseudoelasticity,  on  the  other  hand,  is  an  isothermal  
phenomenon
 
observed
 
when
 
the
 
alloy
 
is
 
deformed
 
above
 
its
 
Af .
 
Upon
 
application
 
of
 
stress,
 
the
 
austenite
 
transforms
 
to
 
stress-induced
 
martensite,
 
accommodating
 
large
 
strains
 
(often
 
several
 
percent).
 
When
 
the
 
stress
 
is
 
removed,
 
the
 
martensite
 
reverts
 
to
 
austenite,
 
and
 
the
 
material
 
recovers
 
its
 
original
 
shape,
 
exhibiting
 
a
 
stress-strain
 
curve
 
with
 
a
 
characteristic
 
hysteresis
 
loop.
11
 This  reversible,  large-strain  deformation  without  
permanent
 
set
 
is
 
highly
 
desirable
 
for
 
various
 
engineering
 
applications.
