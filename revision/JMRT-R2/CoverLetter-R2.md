Dear Editor,

Please find enclosed our second revision of JMRT-D-26-06169R1, *Experimental
Validation of an LLM-Hypothesized Fe-Mn-Al-Ni-Si-C Alloy: Phase Stability Governs
the Absence of Super-elasticity*. Reviewers 1 and 2 closed their reviews (Reviewer 2
recommending acceptance); Reviewer 3 raised eight further points, and this revision
answers each of them. The response to Reviewer 3 quotes every point verbatim and
states where the manuscript changed.

Three things we should draw to your attention directly.

1. **A correction of our own.** In preparing this revision we found that the CALPHAD
   scripts used for Revision 1 had silently omitted the liquid phase from two of the
   three thermodynamic databases, so the α-solvus temperatures quoted in Revision 1
   (≈1340 and ≈1390 °C) were artifacts. Every calculation has been re-run with the
   liquid phase present, the superseded results archived, and every affected sentence
   rewritten (abstract; Secs. 2.6, 3.4, 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 5; Fig. 9 redrawn; new Table 4).
   The corrected result is stronger and simpler — in all three databases the LLM-alloy
   begins to melt before any single-phase α field opens — but the numbers in Revision 1
   were wrong, and we say so in the response (Reviewer 3, point 6).

2. **One attribution corrected in Sec. 3.5.** The 40-minute-anneal elongation quoted in
   Revision 1 (11.0% falling to 1.4%) came from a rod test record that, on re-examination
   of the raw files, belongs to the benchmark heat. It is withdrawn and replaced by the
   LLM-alloy's own record for that condition (25.5% falling to 0.8%), which supports the
   same conclusion. Every tensile record of the LLM-alloy in the prolonged-anneal and
   cyclically treated conditions is now presented directly in a new **Fig. 10**, plotted
   from the raw load–extension exports with each test's gauge stated (Reviewer 3,
   point 7).

3. **Two measurements requested by Reviewer 3 are outstanding.** Reviewer 3 asks for a
   loading–unloading–heating test on the cyclically treated condition (point 7) and a
   direct measurement of the martensite-start temperature (point 8). Neither could be
   completed within the revision period. The manuscript states plainly that neither has
   been performed, and it no longer uses the untested condition or the inferred Ms to
   carry any conclusion. Both measurements have been requested at Fort Wayne Metals.
   Should you consider either necessary for a decision, we would ask for a short
   extension for that item alone, and would report the result as a bound in the terms
   set out in the response.

The other changes are textual: the conclusions of the submitted manuscript are tabulated
as held, corrected, or withdrawn and replaced (point 1); the contribution is stated as
three specific claims and the pre-screening check is explicitly not claimed as new
(point 2); the cyclic heat-treatment schedules are stated per specimen, with the
chronology and the intent (point 3); the gauge length is given per dataset from the
instrument records, and the load-frame record of the Fig. 3 tests has been located
(point 4); and every conclusion is bounded to the conditions reported, with a
three-level statement of what was measured, what is calculated, and what is not
claimed (point 5).

We upload three response documents, one per reviewer. The marked-up manuscript shows
every change against the Revision-1 file the reviewers read.

This work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have
approved the revised version.
