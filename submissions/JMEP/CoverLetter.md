**RE: Submission of Original Research Article — "Mechanical Responses of an AI-Hypothesized Super-elastic Fe-Mn-Al-Ni-Si-C Alloy"**

We are writing to submit our manuscript entitled "Mechanical Responses of an AI-Hypothesized Super-elastic Fe-Mn-Al-Ni-Si-C Alloy" for consideration for publication in the *Journal of Materials Engineering and Performance*.

Large language model (LLM) deep-research agents can now propose new alloy compositions from thermodynamic and literature data in seconds, but whether such AI-proposed compositions survive physical synthesis and deliver their intended properties has rarely been tested experimentally. In this study we used an LLM deep-research agent to hypothesize a low-cost Fe-Mn-Al-Si-Ni-C composition intended to exhibit room-temperature super-elasticity, then synthesized and characterized it under conditions identical to the established benchmark Fe-Mn-Al-Ni alloy of Omori *et al.*

We report what may be the first synthesis and mechanical characterization of such an AI-hypothesized shape memory alloy. The AI-designed alloy was readily castable but showed no super-elastic or shape-memory recovery across every anneal condition tested, behaving instead like a conventional elastically deforming metal — in clear contrast to the benchmark alloy's confirmed stress-induced martensite reversion. Quantitative Rietveld refinement of synchrotron X-ray diffraction data traced this failure to a phase-stability mismatch: the AI alloy forms a γ-FCC-dominated (≈62%) structure with a minor ordered DO₃ Fe₃Al phase in place of the benchmark's coherent, transformation-promoting β-NiAl (B2) precipitates, apparently favored by the AI alloy's elevated carbon content. Alongside this, our anneal-temperature sweep (600–1200 °C) provides a full materials-selection and processing map for the AI alloy — strength, ductility, and modulus as functions of anneal temperature — even though the intended transformation behavior was not achieved.

We believe the *Journal of Materials Engineering and Performance* is well suited to this work: its scope spanning materials selection, processing, characterization, and evaluation matches a study built on composition verification (ICP-AES, ASTM E1019), mechanical testing (cyclic and monotonic tensile), microstructural characterization (optical, SEM/EDS), and synchrotron XRD/Rietveld phase analysis. We believe the combination of a clear engineering-performance narrative (why a specific alloy design did not deliver the intended in-service behavior) with a testable, mechanistic explanation will be of direct interest to this journal's applied-materials-engineering readership.

We confirm that this manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere, and that all authors have read and approved the final manuscript. Google Gemini 2.5 (Deep Research) was used as described in the manuscript itself (Sec. 2.1) to hypothesize the alloy composition under study; this is a study method, not a manuscript-preparation aid. No generative AI tools were used to draft, edit, or otherwise prepare the text of this manuscript.
