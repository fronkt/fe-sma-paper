**RE: Submission of Original Research Article — "Mechanical Responses of an AI-Hypothesized Super-elastic Fe-Mn-Al-Ni-Si-C Alloy"**

We are writing to submit our manuscript entitled "Mechanical Responses of an AI-Hypothesized Super-elastic Fe-Mn-Al-Ni-Si-C Alloy" for consideration as an Original Research Article in *Metallurgical and Materials Transactions A*. This manuscript has not previously been submitted to this journal.

**Findings.** Large language model (LLM) deep-research agents can now propose new alloy compositions from thermodynamic and literature data in seconds, but whether such AI-proposed compositions survive physical synthesis and deliver their intended properties has rarely been tested experimentally. We used an LLM deep-research agent to hypothesize a low-cost Fe-Mn-Al-Si-Ni-C composition intended to exhibit room-temperature super-elasticity, then synthesized and characterized it under conditions identical to the established benchmark Fe-Mn-Al-Ni alloy of Omori *et al.* The AI-designed alloy was readily castable but showed no super-elastic or shape-memory recovery across every anneal condition tested (600–1200 °C), behaving instead like a conventional elastically deforming metal, in clear contrast to the benchmark alloy's confirmed stress-induced martensite reversion under a loading–unloading–heating protocol. Quantitative Rietveld refinement of synchrotron X-ray diffraction data traced this failure to a phase-stability mismatch: the AI alloy forms a γ-FCC-dominated (≈62%) structure with a minor ordered DO₃ Fe₃Al phase in place of the benchmark's coherent, transformation-promoting β-NiAl (B2) precipitates, apparently favored by the AI alloy's elevated carbon content.

**Originality.** To our knowledge, this is the first reported synthesis and mechanical characterization of an Fe-based shape memory alloy composition hypothesized by an LLM deep-research agent, and the first to benchmark such a composition directly against an established super-elastic alloy under identical processing. The physical-metallurgy explanation we develop — phase-fraction imbalance and loss of coherent B2 precipitation, both traceable to a specific compositional choice (elevated carbon) — is, we believe, a novel and directly testable mechanistic account rather than a purely negative result.

**Suitability.** This work sits squarely within this journal's core scope: relationships among processing, structure, and properties in physical metallurgy. The manuscript reports a complete processing–structure–property study of the AI alloy (heat-treatment sweep from 600–1200 °C tracing recovery, recrystallization, and grain growth) alongside phase-level diffraction analysis, and we believe its combination of alloy design, quantitative phase characterization, and mechanistic interpretation will be of direct interest to this journal's readership in the shape memory alloy and physical metallurgy communities.

We confirm that this manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere, and that all authors have read and approved the final manuscript. Google Gemini 2.5 (Deep Research) was used as described in the manuscript itself (Sec. 2.1) to hypothesize the alloy composition under study; this is a study method, not a manuscript-preparation aid. No generative AI tools were used to draft, edit, or otherwise prepare the text of this manuscript. The authors declare no competing interests.
